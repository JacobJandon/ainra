# UK — what would have to change for it to come back into consideration

**This page makes no recommendation.** It exists so that "we looked at the UK" does not decay into folklore. The
five premises behind the original UK case were verified against primary sources in L4 and are recorded in
[`JURISDICTION-CHECKLIST.md`](JURISDICTION-CHECKLIST.md). Two of them did not survive, and they were the two the
case rested on.

This page states, for each, **the precise fact that would have to flip** — so that if it ever does, the answer is
"re-open it", not "start again".

---

## The two that failed

### P2 · The regulator door — the strongest argument, and it is closed as published

The UK digital-verification trust framework defines its own scope in §3.1:

> *"Unless otherwise stated, 'person' refers a natural person, in the sense of an individual human, rather than a
> legal person, like a company."*

and says the narrowing is deliberate, to match the Act it is published under:

> *"To align the trust framework with the scope it is given in the Act, we have also focused the trust framework's
> rules on the identities and attributes of natural persons"*

An AI-agent identity body is not in that scope, and incorporating in the UK does not put it there.

**What would have to flip:** the published scope would have to admit non-human subjects — either a new version of
the framework whose definition of "person" is not limited to individual humans, or a separate published scheme
from the same office covering machine or agent identity. **A blog post, a consultation, or a workshop is not the
flip.** The flip is a published scope document you can read the definition in.

**Deliberately left open, and it matters:** whether an *informal* engagement route exists was **not** verified.
The supplementary codes and the office's annual report published 14 July 2026 were never read. So the honest
statement is *no published route today*, not *no route is possible*. That gap is the cheapest thing on this page
to close, and closing it is not the same as deciding anything.

### P4 · Non-resident director — false, and it bites in both directions

A UK company is UK tax-resident by incorporation:

> *"The incorporation rule at CTA09/S14 states that, with certain exceptions, a UK incorporated company is
> resident in the UK for tax purposes."*

and Bulgaria registers a foreign legal entity whose effective management sits on its territory (BULSTAT Act
art. 3(1)(5)(b)), with a place of management named as a permanent-establishment example (DOPK §1(5)(a)). A UK
company directed from Bulgaria therefore carries UK filing that never goes away **plus** a Bulgarian trigger.

**What would have to flip:** nothing here is a rule that changes on a website. This one flips on **facts about the
operator, not about the UK** — if the person directing the company were UK-resident, the central-management-and-
control question disappears. It is the only premise on this page whose flip is a life change rather than a
publication.

## The one that half-survived

### P1 · Grants — the gate is not incorporation, it is where the work happens

> *"all applicants must be UK-registered companies **and carry out their project activities in the UK**"*

and a foreign company's UK branch is explicitly excluded:

> *"A registration number that does not relate to a fully registered UK business will not be eligible to apply for
> funding, for example FC or BR prefixes."*

So a UK shell run from Bulgaria fails the gate it was created to pass.

**What would have to flip:** either the activities test would have to disappear from the schemes actually being
applied to — verifiable only on the funder's own eligibility page for the specific programme — or the project's
work would have to genuinely happen in the UK, which is the same life change as P4.

## The two that never argued against the UK

**P3 (cost and time)** and **P5 (non-profit form)** both came back workable for the UK: £100 and usually 24 hours
to incorporate, a company limited by guarantee available directly. They were never the reason to go, and they are
not a reason to return. The one thing worth carrying forward from P3 is the prerequisite that appeared in
November 2025: every director now needs a verified-identity personal code **at registration**, and the reliable
cross-border route to one costs money. "Remote and fast" now means *remote and fast once every director has
verified*.

**P5 carries a trap that is worth re-reading before any filing, in any country:** under the Companies Act a
company limited by shares cannot re-register as one limited by guarantee, or the reverse. The share/guarantee
choice is made once, at incorporation. "Incorporate now, convert later" is only true along the routes that exist.

---

## The test, in one line

> **UK re-enters consideration if — and only if — the trust framework publishes a scope that admits non-human
> subjects, or the person directing the entity becomes UK-resident.**

Everything else on this page is detail underneath those two. Neither has happened. No recommendation is made here,
and the decision — including "wait", which the memo insists is a decision and must be written down — stays in
[`JURISDICTION.md`](JURISDICTION.md) § *The decision*, where `make campaign-status` reads it.
